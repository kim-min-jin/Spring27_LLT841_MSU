LLT 841 SPRING 2027

AI for Language Teaching and Research

Mondays, 3:00–6:00 p.m. · Wells Hall A304

This course examines how AI, particularly large language models and generative AI, can support language teaching, learning, and research. The course emphasizes teachers’ and researchers’ agency in selecting, adapting, and evaluating AI to serve clearly defined pedagogical and research goals. Drawing on theories of second language acquisition (SLA), other relevant perspectives, and empirical research, students will critically evaluate AI applications, engage in guided practical activities, and develop their own research study or language teaching design. Students are encouraged to explore applications across languages and adapt their projects to their own linguistic and educational contexts.

No prior programming experience is required.

Course Objectives

By the end of the course, students will be able to:

  • Explain foundational AI and NLP concepts and how LLMs and generative AI systems work.
  • Compare strategies for customizing AI, including prompting, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), and fine-tuning, and select approaches appropriate to specific teaching and research purposes.
  • Use and evaluate AI methods for linguistic analysis, text annotation, feedback, and assessment.
  • Analyze AI-mediated language learning through relevant theories and empirical research.
  • Design and justify an AI-supported research study or language learning activity, specifying its purpose, procedures, and evaluation criteria.
  • Critically evaluate the ethical, social, and educational implications of AI use, including data practices, bias, human agency, and learner development.

Course Requirements

Participation (10%)

Students are expected to closely read at least one assigned reading for each class, review the abstracts, introductions, and conclusions of the remaining readings, and actively contribute to discussions and guided activities. Please notify the instructor before class if you need to miss a session.

Discussion Leading (10%)

Each student will lead one class discussion during the semester on a topic of their choice from the course schedule. Discussion leaders will briefly introduce key issues across the assigned readings and facilitate discussion using prepared questions. Questions should encourage critical examination of research methods, findings, and limitations, as well as connections to students’ teaching and research interests. The emphasis is on facilitating discussion rather than delivering a formal presentation.

Research/Teaching Design Presentation (15%)

Students will present a proposal for an AI-supported research study or language teaching activity on March 29. Presentations should explain the purpose, relevant literature or theoretical rationale, proposed procedures, and evaluation criteria. Students will receive feedback to help refine their final projects. Slides or other supporting materials are required; a separate written report is not required. Presentation length will depend on enrollment.

AI Application and Evaluation Report (15%)

Students will complete a small-scale exploration of an AI application relevant to language teaching or research, such as text annotation, automated feedback, or customized interaction. The report should explain the purpose and procedures, document relevant prompts and settings, evaluate selected outputs using explicit criteria, and discuss limitations and implications. The report should be approximately 1,000 words, excluding references and appendices containing prompts and output examples. Coding or app development is not required. This exploration may inform the design presentation and final project. The report will be due before March 29; the exact deadline will be announced.

Final Project (50%)

Students will develop an AI-supported research project or language teaching design grounded in relevant literature and theory. Research projects should include a clearly defined question, documented methods, and a small-scale analysis or pilot evaluation. Teaching projects should include learning objectives, instructional materials and procedures, and a justified plan for evaluating learning; classroom implementation is not required. All projects should critically address the appropriateness and limitations of the AI approach. Students will present their projects on April 26 and submit a final report during finals week, May 3–7. Further guidelines, including report length and the submission deadline, will be provided.
